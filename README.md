## Why VLIQ

VLIQ follows one rule:

> **Native GNOME behavior first. Change the material, not the desktop interaction model.**

It does not place a second Dock over Ubuntu Dock, recreate Quick Settings, duplicate the calendar, or replace GNOME's controls. GNOME still owns geometry, hit targets, accessibility, input and animation. VLIQ works beneath supported Shell surfaces and changes the material they are painted with.

## VLIQ Atmosphere — Premium Wallpaper

Open **GNOME Extensions → VLIQ → Preferences → Wallpaper**.

Atmosphere changes the wallpaper's material and depth without changing
GNOME's wallpaper files, image URI or desktop color scheme. It is deliberately
**opt-in**: a fresh installation starts at **Original**, with no Atmosphere
shader attached. Existing desktops are unchanged until a preset is chosen.

| Wallpaper style | Look | Filmic · Vignette · Ambient · Grain · Contrast |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Original** (default) | GNOME wallpaper exactly as provided, no extra GPU effect | Off |
| **VLIQ Natural** | Visibly cleaner tones and light corner depth | 38 · 24 · 22 · 2 · 34 |
| **VLIQ Cinematic** | Rich filmic contrast, noticeably deeper corners and tonal separation | 78 · 70 · 34 · 4 · 72 |
| **VLIQ Atmosphere** | Premium filmic lighting, cool/warm ambiance and visible depth | 62 · 52 · 54 · 3 · 56 |
| **Custom** | Adjust each of the five effects independently | Your saved intensities |

A manual slider change selects **Custom**. Returning to **Original**
removes Atmosphere’s GPU effect without deleting your saved levels. The
fine-grain texture is static, not animated. **Natural** is a light filmic
refinement, **Atmosphere** adds noticeable color and depth, and **Cinematic**
provides a deliberately stronger result. These modes never modify the
wallpaper image file or GNOME’s own background settings.

**Visual check:** With your wallpaper in view, switch between **Original**
and **VLIQ Atmosphere**, then between **Original** and **VLIQ Cinematic**.
Look at the outer corners, shadow/midtone depth and warm/cool colors. In
**Wallpaper → Live Wallpaper Renderer**, the *Shader attachment* status
shows the number of actual wallpaper actors that received an effect. A
message such as **Not initialized** or **Not attached** means you should
reload the extension or check GNOME Shell logs, rather than assuming a
shader rendered. **Attached** confirms actor attachment, not final GPU
pixels; device-side visual verification remains required.

### Wallpaper-first scene consistency

GNOME keeps its own wallpaper actor, VLIQ's Liquid Glass uses a distinct
shared wallpaper mirror, and App Grid has its own native blurred wallpaper
actors. Atmosphere hooks **each of these real wallpaper actors once**. It
does **not** tint live Browser windows, App Grid icons, the entire GNOME
`uiGroup`, or the final glass composite. Therefore the same wallpaper
treatment is present on the desktop, in glass wallpaper reflections and in
the App Grid's existing native blur, without stacking Atmosphere twice on
a single source. Native multi-monitor and GNOME wallpaper changes remain
owned by GNOME.

**Performance:** Each active wallpaper paint source receives one static
GPU shader effect (one extra color pass on that source). No CPU screenshots,
texture uploads, timers, frame-based grain or additional App Grid blur layers
are used. It can increase GPU cost versus **Original**; the final GNOME 50
GPU time and color fidelity must be validated on a real device. Previous
Liquid/Frosted glass controls and native popup scene selection are untouched.

## Choose a Glass Material

Open **GNOME Extensions → VLIQ → Preferences → Appearance → Glass Material** and select **Liquid Glass** or **Frosted Crystal**. The mode switches live; it stays selected after logout or restart.

| Material | Appearance | Adjustable controls |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Liquid Glass** (default) | Original v10 refractive lens, chromatic highlights and glossy edge lighting, now with **strong Frosted-like backdrop blur** | Existing Blur slider still controls the stored base radius; tint and Advanced optics remain untouched. |
| **Frosted Crystal** | Dense pearl-white satin diffusion, strongly softened backdrop, subtle 4px micro-etched crystal texture, near-zero rainbow fringe and broad ice-blue edge glow | Frost Density, Micro-grain and Crystal Edge Glow |

At the default Frost Density, the frosted material now masks most fine wallpaper detail instead of resembling a lightly tinted Liquid surface. Its diffuse crystal texture is spatially stable rather than animated. Selecting Liquid restores the original sharp refractive lens and chromatic optics without resetting any Advanced settings. Increasing Frost Density increases matte coverage; lowering it lets more wallpaper detail through.

Switching back to Liquid Glass restores the saved v10 lens, tint and Advanced optics, together with the **new stronger Liquid backdrop blur**. At the default Frost Density (88%), the two modes have comparable background diffusion; Liquid still bends the image at the edges, while Frosted stays matte and nearly achromatic. The original blur slider value is never rewritten. Setting Blur to **0** disables blur in both modes; other slider values are applied through the shared existing blur pipeline with a material-specific boost. Frosted controls are saved separately; neither mode introduces a second overlay or noise texture. Stronger blur can cost more GPU work in **either** mode, so no performance claim is made without a compositor trace.

The material picker applies across supported VLIQ-owned and vendor-native shell surfaces, including the Orb, Spotlight, Dock and top panel glass surfaces, window previews, calendar, Quick Settings and supported popups. Individual native surfaces that intentionally remain non-glass in certain modes (such as Ubuntu Dock's plain panel fallback) keep their established behavior.

### Adaptive icy-blue edges

VLIQ's glass cards use a thin, rounded, icy-blue optical contour rather than a CSS border. The color adapts to the *actual backdrop* (and to the matte body in Frosted Crystal): **soft pale-blue on dark backgrounds** and **very light, low-saturation blue-gray on bright/white backgrounds**. The bright-side color coverage is also reduced, so it reads as a thin premium contour rather than a blue outline. The effect is contained entirely within the existing glass shader's 1–3px rim, with no second layer, no halo outside the card, and no new GPU texture or capture pass.

The original Liquid Glass refraction and Frosted Crystal matte finish still differ; the contour only improves the legibility of both surfaces against contrasting wallpapers. Preview cards retain their compact 480×270 fixed 16:9 layout, and native controls and input remain unchanged.

## Native-first design

<p align="center">
  <img src="docs/graphics/native-vs-vliq.svg" alt="GNOME and VLIQ ownership model" width="100%">
</p>

| Surface | GNOME keeps | VLIQ changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ubuntu Dock / Dash-to-Dock | icons, hit targets, autohide/intellihide, app behavior | supported background/material in header modes; temporarily concealed in Bottom Taskbar mode |
| GNOME panel / Bottom Taskbar | native Activities/workspace toggle, Date/Quick Settings and their menu input | relocated native panel, centered launcher/app shortcuts and glass material in Bottom mode |
| App Grid / Overview | application view, interaction, layout | supported backdrop/material |
| Quick Settings | controls, geometry, interaction | supported Shell material |
| Date / Calendar | calendar, controls, animation | glass material underneath |
| Notifications | content, actions, animation | supported card material |
| Panel / status popups | `PopupMenu` / `BoxPointer` ownership | shared glass material |

VLIQ deliberately leaves GTK/libadwaita application interiors, GNOME accessibility ownership, native hit targets and native animation semantics alone.

**Every native popup follows the real background.** Date/Calendar,
Quick Settings and supported panel/context menus now use **the same
scene-selection policy**, with no wallpaper-only Calendar exception:

- **Super / GNOME Overview / App Grid visible:** the actual native
  `overviewGroup` content, including its icons, workspace/search UI and
  VLIQ's existing blurred background, is captured inside the popup glass.
  The original full-size desktop Browser window is not duplicated.
- **Normal desktop with open apps:** the live, correctly stacked desktop
  windows (Browser, editor, terminal) are rendered over the shared wallpaper,
  so Calendar and Quick Settings both refract **the actual window behind
  them**, not wallpaper alone.
- **Desktop with no overlapping window:** the same shared wallpaper remains
  underneath.

The Overview source is a separate GNOME actor subtree, never the full stage
or the popup's own ancestor, avoiding a self-capture cycle. Source changes
happen in the existing popup frame sync, retain cached-window fallback
compatibility, and use one Liquid/Frosted composite shader. Native input,
Date-menu cards, Quick Settings controls, Dock and 480×270 window previews
remain unchanged.


## Desktop Layout — native Bottom Taskbar

Open **GNOME Extensions → VLIQ → Preferences → Desktop Layout → Desktop layout** and choose one of three live-switchable modes:

| Mode | Layout |
| --- | --- |
| **3-Part Header** (default) | Three separate rounded Liquid Glass islands across the top: Activities, Date and Quick Settings. |
| **Full Header** | GNOME's original full-width top panel with a square Liquid Glass backdrop. |
| **Bottom Taskbar** | Moves the **existing GNOME panel** to the bottom of the primary monitor, with workspace/Activities at the left, **centered VLIQ Start + pinned/running apps**, and Quick Settings + Date/Clock at the right. |

The Bottom Taskbar preserves the native GNOME controls and popup owners instead of drawing a second panel over them. The Start button opens VLIQ's application view. Clicking a pinned app launches it; clicking a running app focuses/restores it, or minimizes a focused window on the next click. Each running app has a centered **5 px round dot** underneath its icon; a stopped pinned app has no visible dot.

**Bottom-only appearance:** one edge-to-edge, borderless Liquid Glass strip. There is no bottom optical rim, dark outline or drop shadow. App hover/pressed states highlight each icon separately; the Date/Clock text is fixed white in this mode. The original Ubuntu Dock (including its background and reactive chrome) is temporarily concealed to prevent a second dark capsule or overlapping click targets. Switching back to either header mode restores the Dock and native panel positions.

**Rendering / lifecycle:** GNOME Shell 50's own `LayoutManager.panelBox` remains the input owner. VLIQ restores bottom placement after native monitor relayout, follows native fullscreen visibility and retains the same `St.Button`/icon actor across app-state changes to avoid click flicker. Dock ancestry is cached for normal frames, while a cheap per-frame visibility guard prevents stale Dock paint; menu arrows use event-driven updates and a bounded fallback. All added actors, signal handlers and temporary settings-side changes are cleaned up on mode exit.

The **Saved layout request** and **Active desktop layout** rows in Preferences report the persisted selection and the running Shell status independently. The bottom layout currently targets **GNOME Shell 50**; other Shell versions have not been verified.

## Manage Ubuntu Dock from VLIQ

Open **GNOME Extensions → VLIQ → Preferences → Dock**. VLIQ
detects the installed Ubuntu Dock settings schema and provides its native
controls directly, without copying or replacing the dock.

The Dock page includes:

- **Visibility:** Always visible, Intelligent hide (window overlap), Auto-hide
  (pointer at the screen edge), pressure-to-reveal, pressure threshold,
  show/hide delays, native slide animation, fullscreen reveal and manual hide.
- **Layout:** native position (top/right/bottom/left), Panel mode, icon size,
  fixed icon size, dock length, center icons, multi-monitor and compact spacing.
- **Content and interactions:** favorites, running apps, Trash/drives, Show
  Applications, native window previews, workspace/monitor filtering,
  click action, wheel action, running indicator style and keyboard shortcuts.
- **Restore Intelligent Auto-hide:** an explicit action to unpin Ubuntu Dock,
  re-enable overlap avoidance and pointer-edge reveal, disable manual hide and
  disable the pressure barrier. It never changes settings just from opening
  the Dock page. Your original custom values remain until you edit them.

**Native ownership:** the controls write to the existing
`org.gnome.shell.extensions.dash-to-dock` GSettings schema, so Ubuntu Dock's
own settings and VLIQ stay in sync. On unsupported installations the page
shows an unavailable message instead of inventing a separate dock. VLIQ
does not call Ubuntu Dock's hide/show animations itself.

**Auto-hide repair:** the GNOME 50 bridge now reads the actual native slider
when no slide override is supplied (the earlier JS `Number(null) === 0`
coercion incorrectly treated the dock as hidden). Intelligent-hide overlap
checks anchor to Ubuntu Dock's stable fully-visible `_staticBox`, instead of
the moving hidden allocation. VLIQ-only vertical offsets adjust that box
consistently. Native preference changes cause one coalesced hover/overlap
reconciliation, not a new polling or hide/show loop.

The existing VLIQ-specific **vertical dock offset** remains at the bottom
of this page and is ignored in Panel mode. Ubuntu Dock and GNOME still own
icons, native edge detection, pressure barriers, window-overlap decisions and
accessibility.

## VLIQ Orb

The Orb can be used as a focused launcher or as a compact full app launcher.

| Source | Contents |
| --- | --- |
| **Dock Apps** | Ubuntu favorites + currently running apps |
| **Custom Apps** | only applications selected in VLIQ Preferences |
| **All Apps** | visible installed applications, alphabetically |

Scroll over the Orb to change pages when the selected source contains more apps than the current layout can display.

Available geometries:

`Orbit` · `Star` · `Molecule` · `Spiral` · `Petal`

<details>
<summary><b>Monitor-safe layout behavior</b></summary>

VLIQ generates the requested geometry, measures the active monitor, translates the layout inward and scales it when necessary. If the complete shape still cannot fit cleanly, VLIQ removes a complete outer level and moves overflow applications to the next page instead of silently dropping individual slots.

This is especially important with **All Apps** near monitor edges and corners.

</details>

## Install

### Requirements

- **GNOME Shell 50** (the only currently declared supported Shell release).
- A compatible Linux desktop running GNOME Shell 50; our primary manual
  testing target is Ubuntu.
- **Ubuntu Dock** is necessary only for its own native Dock-specific options
  and material integration. VLIQ does not ship or install a second Dock.
- GPU compositing for Liquid/Frosted glass and optional Atmosphere effects.

### Install the Store review ZIP (before GNOME publication)

VLIQ is **not yet listed as an approved GNOME extension**. You can test the
same self-contained package we intend to submit for official review:

1. Open [Actions → GNOME Store package](https://github.com/Anuppaul/vliq/actions/workflows/gnome-store-package.yml).
2. Select a successful **`main` branch** run and download the
   **`vliq-gnome-store-upload-zip`** artifact.
3. Extract the artifact wrapper once. Its **inner ZIP** named
   `vliq@wonderer.tech.shell-extension.zip` is the actual GNOME
   extension package.
4. Install the inner ZIP using a GNOME extension manager or:

```bash
gnome-extensions install --force vliq@wonderer.tech.shell-extension.zip
```

Enable **VLIQ** in the GNOME Extensions app. On Wayland, a
**Log Out → Log In** is the recommended way to discover new extensions.

**Do not run the developer installer for a Store/review ZIP.** The upload
package contains standard top-level GSettings XML schemas and all required
runtime files without bundling build scripts or compiled schema binaries.

### Install from source (development / contributors)

```bash
git clone https://github.com/Anuppaul/vliq.git
cd vliq
bash gnome-shell/install.sh
```

This is VLIQ's **developer installer** with local hot-update support.
A clean first install can require **one Log Out → Log In**.

Verify the extension and open Preferences:

```bash
gnome-extensions list --active | grep vliq@wonderer.tech
gnome-extensions prefs vliq@wonderer.tech
```

### Updating

- **Official GNOME Store installation after approval:** use GNOME's normal
  extension update mechanism. The real Store listing will be linked **only
  after GNOME publishes VLIQ**.
- **Store-review ZIP:** install the inner ZIP from a newer successful
  `main` package run, then log out/in if needed.
- **Developer/source installation:** update the source checkout and rerun
  `bash gnome-shell/install.sh`. Hot updates are a developer workflow; a
  Store package must not require them.

### Fullscreen video: top bar and panel visibility

GNOME 50 hides its top bar on a primary-monitor fullscreen window. VLIQ
also keeps its independent **top-panel Liquid Glass backdrop** in sync with
that native visibility while fullscreen software brightness is enabled.
The separate Ubuntu Dock Panel-mode glass backdrop likewise cannot linger
over a fullscreen window; Ubuntu Dock itself retains its existing native
autohide configuration. See [Fullscreen Panel behavior](docs/FULLSCREEN_PANEL.md).

### Quick Settings Brightness — same native slider as Sound

VLIQ shows a Brightness slider immediately **under Sound** in GNOME
Quick Settings. On hardware with a native backlight, it reuses GNOME 50's
existing control. If GNOME hides that control on desktop/external monitors,
VLIQ adds a native-style slider using **software dimming** (30–100%)
of GNOME's full desktop scene—including windows, wallpaper, top panel,
Quick Settings and fullscreen video. When dimming is active, VLIQ
temporarily inhibits Mutter direct scanout so fullscreen video cannot bypass
the brightness effect; this adds GPU compositing work. Brightness 100%
restores the normal direct-scanout optimisation and removes the extra GPU
effect. This does not control the monitor's physical backlight.

Enable/disable via **VLIQ Preferences → Menus → Quick Settings Brightness**.
Restoring the original GNOME layout removes all VLIQ-owned dimming effects.
No external ddcutil, daemon, or extension is required. See
[Quick Settings Brightness guide](docs/QUICK_BRIGHTNESS.md).

### Terminal Glass — native Ptyxis GPU backdrop blur (experimental)

VLIQ now provides **two independent, opt-in layers** for Ptyxis:

- **Native translucency:** the developer-side
  [Ptyxis profile utility](docs/TERMINAL_GLASS.md) backs up and sets only
  background opacity, preserving sharp text.
- **Real compositor background blur:** VLIQ Preferences → **Terminal** →
  **Real backdrop blur**, OFF by default. It uses GNOME's native
  `Shell.BlurEffect(BACKGROUND)` beneath the original Ptyxis surface,
  not a global GTK theme or an additional installed extension.

**Not the full Liquid Glass v10 refractive shader.** Terminal blur
now uses a GNOME 50-native frame rectangle and an original rounded GPU
*output* mask. This specifically targets the blur escaping the window
reported after PR #76, but remains experimental until confirmed on the
GNOME 50.1 desktop. On missing/mismatched geometry or an unavailable
mask shader, it fails closed to the already-working native transparency.
No extra package, GTK global theme or other application is modified. See
the [Terminal Glass guide](docs/TERMINAL_GLASS.md).

```bash
python3 tools/vliq_terminal_glass.py status
```

## Architecture

VLIQ is a **Shell material extension**, not a replacement desktop environment.

The integration model is intentionally small:

```text
runtime.js
├── orbThemeRuntime.js
├── spotlightSearch.js
├── popupGlass.js
├── notificationGlass.js
├── shellCards.js
├── appGridBackdrop.js
└── liquidGlassDock.js
    ├── topBarIslands.js        # 3-Part / Full / Bottom layout lifecycle
    │   └── bottomTaskbar.js    # GNOME panel movement + app buttons
    ├── panelGlass.js          # bounded panel-height glass surfaces
    └── vendor/liquid-glass/
```

### Popup integration

GNOME Shell 50 owns popup geometry through `BoxPointer`. VLIQ inserts its material inside the native popup hierarchy below the original menu content, so GNOME continues to own position, scale, opacity, accessibility and open/close animation.

### Renderer

VLIQ does not use CPU screenshots to fake transparency. The material pipeline uses compositor-native GPU actors/effects with shared wallpaper sources and live compositor clones only where required.

The renderer supports:

- Gaussian and Dual-Kawase blur
- downscaled blur and cache/reuse
- edge displacement / refraction
- chromatic dispersion
- tint, brightness, contrast and saturation
- rim/specular lighting
- optical sampling beyond native card bounds

For the full implementation model, see [docs/ARCHITECTURE.md](docs/ARCHITECTURE.md).

## Performance principles

VLIQ's runtime is designed around a few constraints:

- no permanent JavaScript polling loop for PopupMenu surfaces
- no CPU framebuffer screenshot loop
- shared wallpaper/background sources
- compositor-native Clutter/Mutter rendering paths
- Orb Liquid Glass allocates extra 16-region shader batches only when large app pages need them; hidden batches avoid redundant uniform updates
- cached/downscaled blur where appropriate
- property writes avoided when values have not changed
- hot-path Orb geometry comparison avoids per-frame JSON serialization for static app pages; native Shell-card glass avoids repeated capture-clip and geometry-uniform writes while stationary
- Orb paging reuses the current open-menu app selection, fitted radial layout and repeated page-size slot plans; normal close and explicit changes rebuild them
- up to two inactive Orb pages retain detached button/icon actors only while the menu is open, avoiding re-creating the same icon textures on scroll; cache is released on close, refresh or disable
- unchanged adaptive-text inline styles and stationary Orb capture bounds are not re-applied each refresh
- expensive material created only where required
- unchanged Overview blur settings and search-card capture bounds are not re-applied every refresh
- Overview window captions reuse sampled geometry and skip unchanged color writes; empty close-button glass regions clear just once
- native Overview actor add/remove events invalidate the window-chrome registry; quiet frames skip full-tree scans with a conservative fallback reconciliation
- Ubuntu Dock Panel Mode uses its own strictly **panel-sized LiquidEffect** (VLIQ refraction, micro-grain and 15% milky-white body shade) behind native DockDash icons; 0px square corners and a native fallback remain, without DashManager's full-screen FBO.
- Bottom Taskbar reuses one bounded native-panel glass surface: unchanged app-state signals update existing buttons/dots without recreating icon textures or reordering actors; steady frames skip unnecessary child-order queries.
- Bottom Taskbar caches the native Dock chrome root, hides any reappearing Dock paint/input on the same compositor frame, and reconciles newly added menu arrows by signals plus a bounded 350 ms fallback rather than a full scan every paint.
- native GNOME animation remains responsible for transforms

Performance-sensitive changes should include reproducible before/after evidence where practical.

VLIQ's shared Liquid Glass shader uses a subtle **static micro-grain** and a **15% milky-white body shade** in both flat and refractive paths. The shade is applied before the configurable user tint, without changing glass alpha; no extra overlay actors, GPU textures, or render passes are required. Panel-mode Dock, transparent margins and shadow-only pixels stay unchanged. See the [optional performance capture guide](docs/PERFORMANCE.md) for GNOME Shell CPU/RAM and NVIDIA GPU baseline comparisons.
